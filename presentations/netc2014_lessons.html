McKeachie's Teaching Tips

Image Source: Amazon.com

The Big Picture

  1. Define learning goals
  2. Construct lesson plans
  3. Plan activities
  4. Create presentation materials
  5. Rehearse discussion points
  6. Reflect and modify

1. Define Learning Goals

  • Why is this lesson being taught?
  • What do students know about the topic before the lesson?
  • What should students take away from the lesson?
  • How can student learning be evaluated?

Bloom's Goal Taxonomy

  1. Knowledge
  2. Comprehension
  3. Application
  4. Analysis
  5. Synthesis
  6. Evaluation

Source: McKeachie's Teaching Tips 14th ed, pp10

2. Construct Lessson Plans

  • Refine goals into learning objectives
  • List new concepts, terms, procedures
  • Gather resources for students
  • Define student expectations before and after lesson
  • Outline lesson structure
  • Connect with student experiences

My Lesson Plan Outline

  1. Learning Objectives
  2. New Concepts, Terminology, People
  3. Resources
  4. Students should do Before Class
  5. Students should do After Class
  6. Lesson Setup
  7. Overall Lesson Plan

3. Plan Activities

  • Plan hands-on activities for students
  • 2011 study by UBC shows that students learning complex physics concepts in an interactive lecture environment perform twice as well as students in a traditional lecture environment

Source: University of British Columbia Public Affairs

4. Create Presentation Materials

  • Materials should supplement lesson, but not provide the whole story
  • Less is more
  • Focus on good visuals and key points
  • Include procedural notes for yourself
    Discuss in pairs; check understanding

Presentation Format

  • Creating a comfortable presentation format is key!
  • Reduce distractions and interruptions
  • Smoothly transition between topics
  • Be able to present information in a variety of ways
  • Crisp, clear visuals and text

5. Rehearse Discussion Points

  • Any in-class discussion requires massive amounts of preparation
  • What topic should be discussed?
  • What format should be used?
  • Starters: questions, case studies
  • Ways to share results
  • Getting 100% involvement (and avoiding monopolies)

6. Reflect and Modify

  • Take time after the class to reflect
    • What went well?
    • What went wrong?
    • What did students respond well to?
    • What did students struggle with?
    • What came up in the discussion?
  • Modify your lesson plan ASAP

What Students Like

  • Openly ask questions without judgement
  • Clear, timely feedback
  • Suggestions to improve
  • Multiple venues for additional help
  • Be consistent in expectations

What Students Dislike

  • Not being able to see the big picture
  • Learning activities without a clear objective
  • Bad grades without feedback
  • Single-minded approach to teaching and learning
  • Difficulty in obtaining information

Tips for Teachers

  • First impressions really do matter!
  • Be prepared, but don't over-rehearse
  • Be flexible: each class is different
  • Learn how each student learns best
  • Take care of yourself

Discussion Time!

  • Think of your favorite teacher. What did she or he do that you liked?
  • Think of your least favorite learning experience. Why was it so bad?
  • What are your best/worst experiences being the teacher?
